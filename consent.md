# Swap pilot: consent to share card data

**Run by:** Michael Wang (NYU student project, not a company)
**Dates:** link in early November 2026, test week Nov 8-14, data deleted by Nov 30, 2026

## What this is
A one-week test of whether weekly texts and small swaps help NYU students spend less on food and getting around. It's for a product case study.

## What you'll do
1. Link **one card** (the one you use most for food and rides) on Michael's laptop through Plaid, a bank-connection service many finance apps use.
2. Name one thing you'd like to save for.
3. Depending on your group, either get a short text from Michael on Sunday and text him one word when you buy food or a ride that week, **or** do nothing else (comparison group).

## What's collected
- **Kept:** only food, drink and getting-around charges from the last ~13 months and the test week: restaurants, bars, coffee, quick food, delivery, groceries, Uber/Lyft and similar rides, subway, bikes. For each: date, merchant name, amount, category.
- **Never saved:** everything else, including rent, shopping, paychecks, transfers, Venmo/Zelle, account numbers, balances, locations. The script drops these before anything is written to disk.
- Michael never sees your bank login. You type it into Plaid yourself.
- Plaid's privacy policy: https://plaid.com/legal/#end-user-privacy-policy

## Who sees it
- Only Michael. Your data is stored on his laptop under a code like "p03," not your name.
- Anything published (case study, portfolio, GitHub) uses group totals or pseudonyms. Quotes from you only with your OK.
- Michael won't bring up your spending around other people.

## Leaving and deletion
- You can quit anytime by texting "stop." Your bank link is disconnected and your data deleted that day.
- When the test ends, every bank link is disconnected and all data deleted by **Nov 30, 2026**.

## Risks
Your purchase history is personal. The main risk is someone seeing it, so it stays on one laptop and is never shared. No money moves and nothing is charged.
